\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A common technique in machine learning is to point a network at a
target direction in feature space---a known semantic concept such as
sentiment polarity, the truthfulness of a statement, or the valence
of an emotion---and add an \emph{auxiliary loss} that pulls the
network's penultimate features toward this direction. The recipes
vary widely. Knowledge distillation (KD)~\citep{hinton2015distilling}
aligns a student network's features with those of a teacher network.
Representational similarity analysis (RSA)~\citep{kriegeskorte2008rsa}
aligns the geometry of pairwise distances between two networks.
Contrastive losses~\citep{khosla2020supcon,vandenoord2018cpc} pull
same-class features together and push different-class features apart.
Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) adapters such as
LoRA~\citep{hu2022lora} restrict the network's update to a low-rank
direction in weight space. Channel-targeted topographic
losses~\citep{padakanti2025meg} bias which input channels matter most. The shared assumption across all
of these methods is that the auxiliary loss provides information the
task labels alone cannot.

We test 25 such techniques on FACED~\citep{chen2023faced}, a public
dataset of EEG recordings from $123$ subjects watching $28$ emotional
video clips ($32$ EEG channels at $250$\,Hz, $9$ emotion classes).
None help. Sixteen produce statistically significant accuracy
decrements (change in balanced accuracy $\Delta$BACC $\le -0.013$, the
fraction-correct averaged uniformly across the $9$ classes;
$p<0.05$, paired across five seeds); zero produce gains. Five of the $25$ recipes show
\emph{monotonic destruction}: the harder we push the auxiliary loss,
the worse accuracy gets, with no positive setpoint to find. The
transition from neutral-to-harmful is sharp: an auxiliary loss that
has no measurable effect at a weak baseline (balanced accuracy $\le
0.62$) becomes a statistically significant negative once the baseline
strengthens (BACC $\ge 0.66$). The same intervention can be a no-op
on one recipe and a meaningful regression on the next, separated by
a few points of headline accuracy.

We call this regularity \emph{saturation}, and it is not the same
thing as overfitting. Overfitting is a network memorising its
training set. Saturation is what happens when the network has
\emph{already} learned the concept the auxiliary loss was meant to
teach, from the task labels alone. Once the EEG classifier has
converged on the 9-class emotion task, its penultimate features are
organised so that each emotion's trials cluster around a stable mean
position; the average direction in feature space along which positive
and negative emotions sit on opposite sides---the
\emph{class-mean component}---is already the target concept. The
auxiliary loss can only deform this already-correct direction. The
remaining variation, which lives in the directions \emph{within} each
class (the \emph{within-class residual}), is what discriminates
between trials of the same emotion and what an additional seed could
plausibly improve. The auxiliary loss does not reach this
within-class residual. Pushing harder on the loss therefore moves
the saturated component further along the target direction without
adding any new discriminative signal, and accuracy drops.

The same picture suggests a positive prescription: rather than
supervise the saturated component, ensemble across multiple training
seeds whose within-class residuals differ, recovering the gain that
auxiliary supervision cannot. Following this prescription on FACED-9
yields a new state of the art at $\mathbf{0.6948}$ balanced accuracy
on a $10$-checkpoint softmax ensemble (best val-selected single
checkpoint $\mathbf{0.6755}$), a $+10.5\%$ relative improvement over
EMOD~\citep{chen2025emod}, the previous FACED-9 SOTA at $0.6287$.
Surprisingly, the class prototypes used by the knowledge-distillation
step in our recipe can be replaced by random orthonormal vectors
without measurable degradation ($\Delta$BACC $\le 0.003$). The SOTA
gain is therefore \emph{structural}: the loss imposes a useful
9-class shape on the features regardless of which direction the
prototypes point in.

This raises a tension that the rest of the paper resolves directly:
if random prototypes give the same gain as language-model-derived
ones, what is a language-model-derived direction doing in this paper
at all? It serves as a probe of \emph{which} concept the network has
saturated on, demonstrating that saturation operates on a real
semantic axis rather than on noise. The construction uses nine
sentences. We write one short emotion-evocative story per FACED
class, pass each through a language model (we use Qwen3-4B as the
lead model), and take the first principal component of the model's
late-layer features across the nine resulting story representations.
We call this one-dimensional direction the \emph{valence axis} (or
V-axis for short), oriented so that the centroid of the Joy story
projects to a positive value. Figure~\ref{fig:universal-vaxis} summarises the three external-validity
results in one panel each. \textbf{(a)} The same direction predicts
movie-review sentiment zero-shot on SST-2~\citep{socher2013recursive},
a standard benchmark of $\sim$$10$k labelled film reviews, at AUC
$0.832$ from a single Qwen3-4B run. \textbf{(b)} It predicts the EEG
response averaged across $123$ FACED subjects: a single ridge
regression on all $160$ channel-band features matches the Qwen3-4B
V-axis projection at $r=0.80$ over the $28$ video clips
($p<10^{-5}$); a CLIP-text variant of the V-axis built from the same
$28$ stimulus descriptions reaches $r=0.87$ on the same ridge
($p<10^{-9}$).
\textbf{(c)} It emerges spontaneously in EEG classifiers: across $36$
checkpoints trained only on the $9$ emotion labels, the alignment
between the network's class-mean component and the V-axis predicts
balanced accuracy at $r=+0.885$.
The probe demonstrates the concept is real (the saturation operates
on a genuine semantic axis); the SOTA prescription shows that, once
the network has saturated, only the loss's structure matters, not the
direction it points in.

\paragraph{Contributions.} \textbf{(1)} The \emph{saturation
regularity}: across $25$ representation-alignment recipes on FACED-9,
$16$ produce statistically significant accuracy decrements and $0$
produce gains, with monotonic destruction in $5$ families and a
sharp transition in $[0.62, 0.66]$ balanced accuracy. A direct
mechanism check shows that the auxiliary loss moves the saturated
class-mean component by $\Delta|r|=+0.01$ to $+0.36$ but moves the
within-class residual by $\sim 10^{-7}$, a numerical zero
(\S\ref{sec:saturation}). \textbf{(2)} A residual-diversity ensemble
that turns the mechanism into a positive prescription: per-checkpoint
within-class residual encoding predicts leave-one-out ensemble
contribution at $r=+0.74$, and a $10$-checkpoint softmax ensemble
reaches FACED-9 SOTA at $\mathbf{0.6948}$
(\S\ref{sec:convergence},~\S\ref{sec:sota}). \textbf{(3)} The
\emph{V-axis probe}: a one-dimensional valence direction extracted
from $9$ language-model emotion stories that simultaneously predicts
text sentiment, predicts cohort EEG response, and emerges
spontaneously in EEG classifiers
(\S\ref{sec:vaxis-llms}--\S\ref{sec:topography}). All three
contributions robustness-tested on a second EEG dataset,
SEED-V~\citep{liu2022seedv} (Appendix~\ref{app:seedv-full}).

Limitations and scope statements are catalogued in
