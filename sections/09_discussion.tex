\section{Discussion: Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:discussion}

Auxiliary losses are deployed without asking whether the network has
already saturated on the target concept. Our results---a saturation
regularity organising $25$ recipe failures, a residual-diversity
ensemble that turns the mechanism into FACED-9 SOTA, and a nine-story
V-axis probe---are scoped to FACED-9 with a measurable V-axis substrate.
The probe transfers are direction-existence results, not neuroscience
mechanism claims; the posterior topography speaks to the video-viewing
paradigm, not to the frontal-asymmetry
account~\citep{davidson1992anterior}; and the cohort signal is
population-level, not within-subject
(Appendix~\ref{app:topography-deep-dive}). Three limitations bound the
broader reading. First, we do not claim architecture-independent
convergence: a V-axis-encoding-versus-accuracy correlation pooled
across backbones is pseudo-replicated (it collapses within any single
architecture and fails a matched-norm null), so convergence is scoped
to seeds of one architecture. Second, the cross-model similarity of the
LLM valence geometry is largely inherited from shared input structure
rather than learned---an untrained model reproduces it---so we treat
the V-axis as a convenient probe, not a universal learned
representation. Third, the transfer is content-specific (valence
carries across text, vision, and EEG; arousal only in vision), the
weak-to-converged transition is cleanest on
CIFAR-10~\citep{krizhevsky2009cifar} (text shows content-irrelevance
but not a significant help-when-weak effect), and
the EmoEEG-MC confound control is inconclusive. The practical corollary
is that aux-loss design papers should report the receiving baseline's
saturation state before attributing a gain to the loss. Whether
the residual-vs-basin decomposition generalises to other saturated
concepts---truthfulness, refusal, sentiment polarity---is the question
this paper opens.
