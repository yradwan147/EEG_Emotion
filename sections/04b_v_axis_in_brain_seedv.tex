\section{Cross-Dataset Replication: SEED-V}
\label{sec:vaxis-brain-seedv}

A second EEG dataset, SEED-V~\citep{liu2022seedv}, lets us re-test
every claim with no parameter shared with FACED. We re-build the
V-axis from scratch on its five emotion classes ($16$ subjects,
$62$ channels), and re-run the four claims in turn.

\paragraph{Probe predicts cohort EEG (replicates).}
The cohort EEG--LLM signal returns at $r{=}{+}0.6159$ ($p<10^{-5}$)
at the P1/$\theta$ cell, with the same posterior-dominant gradient
(occipital $-$ frontal $+0.076$).

\paragraph{Saturation (replicates).}
Adding the V-axis as supervision moves accuracy by a margin within
seed noise ($\Delta{=}{+}0.0015$ across $5$ baseline and $5$ V-axis
seeds; full numerics in Appendix~\ref{app:seedv-full}), at a baseline
strength below the FACED transition---the same neutrality on weak
baselines we report on FACED.

\paragraph{Mechanism (replicates harder).}
Across $15$ stock CBraMod SEED-V checkpoints, accuracy correlates
with V-axis encoding strength at $r{=}{+}0.601$ ($p{=}0.018$)---a
correlation built on a different EEG dataset, a different label
space, and a different best cell, with the same sign and magnitude
as FACED. The directional ablation replicates harder still:
projecting the V-axis residual out of the penultimate features at
inference time drops accuracy on every one of the $15$ checkpoints
($\Delta\mathrm{BACC} \in [-0.029, -0.009]$, mean $-0.0173$, a
$3.9\%$ relative drop on the $\sim 0.44$ baseline against $2.4\%$
on FACED), at mean $z{=}{+}23.6$ above a matched random-direction
null. No checkpoint survives the null at $p<0.001$, parametric
one-sided. Full Step 1--5 numerics, FAA bars, and per-checkpoint
details are in Appendix~\ref{app:seedv-full}.
