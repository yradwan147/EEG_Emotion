\section{The Saturation Regularity}
\label{sec:saturation}

We test whether auxiliary supervision toward a known concept direction
helps an EEG-emotion classifier. The setup uses an
EMOD~\citep{chen2025emod} backbone (the previous FACED-9 SOTA) at
two strengths we call \emph{weak} (network depth $d{=}3$, $0.62$ BACC)
and \emph{converged} ($d{=}6$, the SOTA recipe of \S\ref{sec:sota},
$0.658$ BACC 5-seed mean; best single $0.6755$). The $25$-recipe screen
ran on the weak baseline; strongest cells
were re-evaluated on the converged recipe to localise the transition in
$[0.62, 0.66]$. Each
intervention adds an auxiliary loss that pulls the network's
penultimate features toward a target direction. Targets span the nine LLM-derived class prototypes and their first
principal component, the 1-D V-axis (\S\ref{sec:vaxis-llms}); CLIP-text
embeddings; and frontal-channel masks matched to the analytical valence
ceiling (the maximum V-axis alignment attainable on FACED). Loss families span
knowledge distillation~\citep{hinton2015distilling}, representational
similarity analysis (RSA)~\citep{kriegeskorte2008rsa}, contrastive
losses, parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT)
adapters~\citep{hu2022lora}, curriculum schedules, multi-LLM
ensembles, channel-targeted topographic losses, and anger-weighted
variants (full table in Appendix~\ref{app:full-vaxis-table}).

\paragraph{Result.} None help. Fifteen produce statistically
significant accuracy decrements ($p<0.05$, paired across five seeds);
zero produce gains. Five families show \emph{monotonic destruction}:
the harder we push the auxiliary loss, the worse accuracy gets, with
no positive setpoint. Even the anger-weighted target that
analytically maximises the V-axis ceiling on FACED is among the worst
($\Delta\mathrm{BACC} = -0.054$): supplying the strongest possible
target makes things strictly worse, not better.

\paragraph{The transition is sharp and unidirectional in baseline
strength.} An intervention whose effect lies within seed noise on a
weak baseline (BACC $\le 0.62$) becomes a statistically significant
negative on the converged recipe ($0.658$ BACC); the weak-vs-converged
difference is a significant backbone$\times$loss interaction (Welch
$p{=}0.001$, matched variant). The transition sits cleanly in the band
$[0.62, 0.66]$ on FACED-9 (we make no claim about universal cut-offs).

\paragraph{The regularity generalises beyond valence and beyond EEG.}
An \emph{arousal} target (the same recipe re-anchored on arousal poles)
hurts the converged backbone just as much (topographic
$\Delta\mathrm{BACC}={-}0.031$, $p{=}0.011$; emod-style ${-}0.035$,
$p{=}0.007$; Holm-corrected) while remaining within seed noise on the
weak $d{=}3$ baseline---two concept axes, one saturation
(Figure~\ref{fig:saturation-transition}b). The weak-to-converged
transition also reproduces outside EEG: on CIFAR-10~\citep{krizhevsky2009cifar} an auxiliary head
significantly \emph{improves} an undertrained backbone (Holm
$p\in[0.006,0.016]$) and the benefit vanishes at convergence
($p{>}0.37$; Figure~\ref{fig:saturation-transition}a).
Across $20$ further non-EEG branches (text, vision, audio) an aligned
target never significantly beats a random one ($0/20$): what a
converged network rejects, and a weak one accepts, is the loss's
\emph{structure}, not its \emph{content}
(Appendix~\ref{app:saturation-extras}).

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Saturation (empirical regularity; FACED-9, EMOD backbone).}
\emph{For a FACED-9 classifier $f_\theta$ whose task-only
$\mathrm{BACC}(\theta)$ lies in the band $[0.62, 0.66]$ or higher, adding
any V-axis auxiliary loss $\mathcal{L}_v$ at $\lambda > 0$ produces
$\mathbb{E}[\Delta\mathrm{BACC}] \leq 0$ across all families tested
($15/25$ significant at $p<0.05$, $0/25$ positive). The threshold
coincides with the regime where the V-axis encoding strength---the
absolute correlation $|r|$ between the class-mean features and the
V-axis---saturates across seeds (\S\ref{sec:convergence}). This is an
empirical regularity over the recipes tested, not a proved theorem.}
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Saturation is not overfitting.} Overfitting is memorisation
of the training set. Saturation is a property of how task and
auxiliary supervision interact once both target the same direction
in feature space. The mechanism check below makes the distinction
concrete.

\paragraph{Mechanism check.} The auxiliary loss does push the network's
class-mean features further along the target direction (alignment
increases by $\Delta|r|=+0.01$ to $+0.36$), yet this buys no
generalization: with the loss active, training accuracy stays pinned
at ceiling while test balanced accuracy \emph{falls} on the converged
recipe ($0.658\!\to\!0.629$; Appendix~\ref{app:full-vaxis-table}). The added supervision reshapes a representation
the network has already converged onto from task labels alone, rather
than supplying new discriminative signal. What actually carries
generalization is the \emph{within-class residual}---the directions
that distinguish one trial of an emotion from another---which is
seed-specific and load-bearing: its per-checkpoint encoding strength
predicts leave-one-out ensemble contribution at $r{=}{+}0.74$
(\S\ref{sec:sota}). Pushing the already-saturated class-mean basin
along a fixed target does not, at the class-mean level, improve how the
network uses this residual.
The $25$ recipes reduce to one principle: \emph{a saturated
representation is an unhelpful supervision target}, qualitatively
similar to the few-class distillation
regime~\citep{muller2020subclass,loo2024lelp,yuan2020revisitkd},
here with the residual-variance mechanism made explicit. Full table,
transition table, and the alternative-target branch's failure
($\Delta\in[-0.0193,-0.0145]$) in
Appendix~\ref{app:full-vaxis-table}--\ref{app:saturation-extras}.
