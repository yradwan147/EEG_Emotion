\section{Discussion: Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:discussion}

Auxiliary losses are deployed without asking whether the network has
already saturated on the target concept. Our three results---a
saturation regularity organising $25$ recipe failures, a
residual-diversity ensemble that turns the mechanism into FACED-9
SOTA, and a nine-story V-axis probe---share one scope: the regularity
is demonstrated on FACED-9 with a measurable V-axis substrate, and two
of its boundaries are deliberate rather than gaps. The probe transfers
are direction-existence results, not neuroscience mechanism claims;
the posterior topography is a statement about the video-viewing
paradigm, which by construction does not speak to the frontal-asymmetry
account~\citep{davidson1992anterior} measured under different
paradigms. The transfer is also content-specific---valence carries
across text, vision, and EEG but arousal only in vision
(Appendix~\ref{app:specificity-controls})---and the cohort signal is
population-level: a $9$-stimulus emotional-pole contrast where a
per-subject channel oracle reaches $\overline{|r|}=0.616$ versus
$0.021$ at cohort-fixed channels
(Appendix~\ref{app:topography-deep-dive}). The practical corollary is
that aux-loss design papers should report
the receiving baseline's saturation state, since the same loss can be
within seed noise on a $0.62$ recipe and a significant negative on a
$0.66$ recipe (\S\ref{sec:saturation}). Whether the residual-vs-basin
decomposition generalises beyond saturated 9-class EEG---and to other
saturated concepts such as truthfulness, refusal, or sentiment
polarity---is the question this paper opens.
