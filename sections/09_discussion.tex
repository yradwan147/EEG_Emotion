\section{Discussion: Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:discussion}

Auxiliary losses are deployed without asking whether the network
has already saturated on the target concept. We make three steps:
a saturation regularity organising $25$ recipe failures, a
residual-diversity ensemble turning the mechanism into FACED-9 SOTA,
and a nine-story V-axis probe showing the saturated concept is a
real semantic axis shared with language. The regularity is scoped
to FACED-9 with a V-axis substrate; probe transfers are
direction-existence rather than neuroscience claims; the posterior
topography is a video-paradigm statement, not a refutation of
FAA~\citep{davidson1992anterior}. The cross-modal asymmetry (valence
transfers across text, vision, and EEG; arousal transfers in vision
but collapses in text) suggests the saturating concept is itself
content-specific rather than recipe-universal: the same PCA-on-LM
construction that works for valence is not a generic affective-axis
recipe (Appendix~\ref{app:specificity-controls}). The same diagnosis
predicts where on the population-vs-individual spectrum further gains
should appear: the cohort signal lives in a 9-stimulus
emotional-pole contrast (Appendix~\ref{app:topography-deep-dive}),
while a per-subject channel-selection oracle reaches
$\overline{|r|}=0.616$ versus $0.021$ at the cohort-fixed channels.
A practical corollary
follows for the EEG-emotion community: aux-loss design papers should
report the saturation state of the receiving baseline, since the same
loss that is within seed noise on a $0.62$ recipe can be a significant
negative on a $0.66$ recipe by the same absolute margin
(\S\ref{sec:saturation}, Appendix~\ref{app:full-vaxis-table}) --- a
comparison currently obscured when only the headline number is
disclosed. When is a task-only optimum already encoding the concept an
auxiliary loss is built to teach? Per-subject residual adaptation
(Appendix~\ref{app:discussion-extras}) is the immediate test;
adapting the V-axis substrate to other concepts the network has
similarly saturated on (truthfulness, refusal, sentiment polarity in
text classifiers) is the broader generalisation we leave open. The
$+10.5\%$ FACED gain came from a residual we could measure but not
supervise; whether the same residual-vs-basin decomposition is the
right design rule beyond saturated 9-class EEG --- on saturated tasks
in language and vision where supervision cost is comparable to
ensembling cost --- is the question this paper opens. The
saturation regularity itself is empirical, not theoretical: we
characterise it on $25$ recipes spanning five loss families and
verify the unidirectional sign-flip across two FACED-9 baselines, but
a closed-form prediction of the threshold $\rho^*$ from architecture
and data alone is left to future work.
