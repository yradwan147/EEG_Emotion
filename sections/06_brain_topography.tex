\section{Brain Topography: A Posterior-Visual Scope Statement}
\label{sec:topography}

Having found the saturated valence axis in the cohort EEG
(\S\ref{sec:vaxis-brain}), we close the probe by bounding \emph{where}
that signal lives and what it is not---a scope statement, not a
mechanistic claim.
The cohort signal is posterior-dominant: region-mean correlation is
strongest at occipital electrodes ($|r|=0.21$) and weakest at frontal
($|r|=0.16$), with the largest single cell at PO3/$\gamma$ ($r=+0.48$;
Figure~\ref{fig:topomap-5band}). The signal is carried by an
\emph{anger-versus-warm-positive} contrast on $9$ of the $28$ video
clips: cohort $r{=}+0.87$ at PO3/$\gamma$ for Anger, Amusement, and
Tenderness, against $r{=}-0.02$ on the remaining $19$ mid-valence
stimuli. Removing Anger alone shrinks the cohort correlation to
$+0.33$. Full per-region tables, the Simpson's-paradox per-subject
decomposition, time-resolved late-positive-potential (LPP) window dynamics, and theta--gamma
phase-amplitude coupling tests are in
Appendix~\ref{app:topography-deep-dive} (five-band scalp topomaps in
Figure~\ref{fig:topomap-5band}).

\paragraph{This is not a refutation of frontal-alpha asymmetry
(FAA).} Davidson's FAA~\citep{davidson1992anterior,coan2004frontal} was
developed on static stimuli; our cohort signal is on dynamic video,
where right-minus-left frontal asymmetry stays positive but small
($+0.006$ to $+0.015$ across F-pairs). We claim only that, on dynamic
video, the cohort signal aligned with the LLM direction is carried by
visual-content processing of high-arousal clips---not that the V-axis
subsumes FAA or that posterior cortex is the seat of valence.
Within-subject FAA is a separate question this analysis does not
address.
