\section{Brain Topography: A Posterior-Visual Scope Statement}
\label{sec:topography}

The cohort signal is posterior-dominant. Region-mean $|r|$ is $0.212$
in occipital channels (Oz, O1, O2, POZ, PO3, PO4), $0.181$ in
parietal, $0.180$ in central, and $0.162$ in frontal (FP1, FP2, FPZ,
F3, F4, FZ, F7, F8); the strongest single cells are PO3/$\gamma$
($r{=}{+}0.48$), F7/$\beta$ ($-0.47$), and O1/$\gamma$ ($+0.44$),
with the dominant gradient running posterior-to-anterior
(Figure~\ref{fig:topomap-5band}).

\paragraph{This is not a refutation of frontal-alpha asymmetry.}
Davidson's frontal-alpha asymmetry
hypothesis~\citep{davidson1992anterior,coan2004frontal} was developed
on static stimuli; the cohort signal we report is on dynamic
$28$-second video. Right-minus-left frontal-alpha asymmetry remains
positive in our data (F4-F3 $+0.0062$, F8-F7 $+0.0155$, Fp2-Fp1
$+0.0116$), consistent with FAA's predicted direction; the largest
single frontal asymmetry is at Fp2-Fp1/$\delta$ ($+0.044$), with
canonical alpha asymmetries smaller than the posterior $|r|$
band-to-band. This is a paradigm-level finding for video-evoked
emotion: in the dynamic-video regime the cohort signal lives where
the visual content does, not where Davidson's theory of static-image
approach/withdrawal predicts. Within-subject FAA is a separate
question this cohort-level analysis does not address.

\paragraph{The signal is an anger-versus-warm-positive contrast.}
The cohort signal is not a smooth gradient: it is an
\emph{anger-versus-warm-positive} contrast carried by nine of the
$28$ clips. Cohort $r{=}{+}0.870$ at PO3/$\gamma$ for Anger,
Amusement, and Tenderness, against $r{=}{-}0.015$ for the remaining
$19$ mid-valence stimuli. Removing Anger alone shrinks the cohort
correlation to $+0.327$. The between-subject effect coexists with a
Simpson's paradox at the per-subject level (cohort $r{=}0.48$ vs.\
per-subject $-0.062$, oracle
$|r|{=}0.616$~\citep{sani2024crosssubject}); time-resolved peaks
coincide with classical EEG markers in the LPP
window~\citep{hajcak2010ofthelpp,codispoti2023arousal}; theta-gamma
phase-amplitude coupling does not mediate the
V-axis~\citep{canolty2010cfc}. We do not claim the V-axis subsumes
FAA or that posterior visual cortex is the seat of valence; we claim
that, on dynamic video, the cohort signal that aligns with the
LLM-derived direction is carried by visual-content processing of a
subset of high-arousal clips. Full caveats and time-resolved analysis
are in Appendix~\ref{app:topography-deep-dive}.
