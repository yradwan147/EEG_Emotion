\begin{abstract}
We study what happens when an explicit concept direction is added as
auxiliary supervision to a network that has already learned the same
concept from task labels alone. Across $25$ representation-alignment
interventions on FACED 9-class EEG emotion classification---knowledge
distillation, RSA, contrastive losses, PEFT adapters, and
channel-targeted topographic losses---none help: $16$ produce
statistically significant accuracy decrements, and $0$ produce gains.
We call this regularity \emph{saturation}: above an empirical
threshold (FACED-9 balanced accuracy in $[0.62, 0.66]$), the concept
direction is already the geometry the network finds from task labels,
and the auxiliary loss perturbs that saturated component while leaving
the orthogonal within-class residual---the load-bearing
subspace---untouched. Saturation is distinct from overfitting: it is
not memorisation, but a property of how task supervision and auxiliary
supervision interact once they target the same direction. The same
mechanism gives a positive prescription: ensemble across the residual
rather than supervise the basin. Following this prescription on
FACED-9 yields a new state of the art at $\mathbf{0.6948}$ balanced
accuracy ($+10.5\%$ over EMOD's $0.6287$), with the surprising
property that the LLM-derived class prototypes used in our recipe are
interchangeable with random orthonormal directions ($\Delta\le 0.003$):
the gain is structural, not semantic. As a probe of the concept the
network saturates on, we extract a one-dimensional \emph{valence
direction} by PCA from the late-layer activations of nine short
LLM-generated emotion stories; the same direction recovers SST-2
sentiment zero-shot at AUC $0.832$ from a single Qwen3-4B run,
predicts cohort-averaged EEG of $123$ subjects watching emotional
videos at $r{=}0.87$, and emerges spontaneously in $36$ EEG
classifiers trained only on the 9-class label. All claims replicate on
SEED-V.
\end{abstract}
